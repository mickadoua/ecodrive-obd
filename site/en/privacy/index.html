In short

  • Your vehicle identification number (VIN) is never saved and never sent.
  • Three third-party services may receive location data when you use certain features. They are described below.

What the app reads

From the car, through the Bluetooth OBD-II (ELM327) adapter: engine speed, vehicle speed, air flow, fuel flow, time since engine start, fuel type. The app uses them to compute your fuel consumption and your eco-driving score.

The VIN (vehicle identification number). The app reads it once when it connects, to work out the make and model year. After that, the full number is discarded. It is written to no file and sent to no one. Only the make and year stay in memory while connected, to be displayed.

On the phone:

  • Your GPS position (latitude, longitude, altitude), once per second, during a trip.
  • Atmospheric pressure, if your phone has a barometer, to measure altitude.
  • The name and Bluetooth address of the OBD adapter you choose, so the app can reconnect to it next time, and, if you turn on auto-start, those of the car.
  • The vehicle details you type in yourself (make, model, year, fuel, engine size, segment).

Location: why and when

Location is used for two things: measuring the slope of the road (so you are not penalised on hills) and drawing a map, road by road, of where you use more fuel than expected.

It is read while the app is connected to the OBD adapter (in practice, while the car's ignition is on). This continues when the app is in the background or the screen is off: a "Trip in progress" notification stays visible during that time. Reading stops when the connection to the OBD adapter ends.

A trip can also start without you opening the app, if you have turned on "Automatic start": when your phone connects to your car's Bluetooth, the app connects to the OBD adapter and records the trip. This is the only reason it asks for the "Allow all the time" location permission. This feature is off until you turn it on, and you can withdraw the permission at any time.

Location access is optional. Without it, the app still works, but without the map or the slope correction. You can remove it at any time in Android settings.

What leaves your phone, and where it goes

We send nothing to a server we own. These are the only network exchanges the app makes, and all relate to location.

1. Open-Meteo (elevation) — only if your phone has no barometer.

  • Data sent: the GPS coordinates of your trip (5 decimals, about 1 metre), in batches of at most 100 points, as the trip goes on, to get ground elevation. As with any internet connection, the service can see your IP address.
  • If your phone has a barometer, this exchange does not happen.
  • Encrypted connection (HTTPS).
  • On their side: Open-Meteo states that it keeps server logs, which may contain coordinates, for 90 days and does not share them. Their policy: https://open-meteo.com/en/terms (read on 15 October 2026). Those are their rules, not ours.

2. OpenStreetMap (map background) — when you open the Map tab.

  • Data sent: requests for map tiles of the area you are viewing. That area shows where you are or where you drove. The servers can see your IP address. The app identifier (tech.dm.conso) is included in the request.
  • On their side: the OpenStreetMap Foundation runs these servers. Its policy: https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy (read on 15 October 2026).
  • Tiles already loaded are kept in the app's cache to avoid downloading them again.

3. Android's street-name service — when you open the map or a day's detail.

  • Data sent: the coordinates of one point on each stretch of road, to get the street name. The app does not contact this service itself: it uses Android's geocoding feature. On most phones this service is provided by Google and follows Google's rules. On a phone without Google services it may not work, and street names are then not shown.
  • Only the street name is kept in the app. Not the coordinates that were sent.

We do not sell any data. We receive none.

Android backup

Android may back up the app's data to your Google account if backup is turned on on your phone. This backup is managed by Android and Google, not by us. You control it in your phone settings (System > Backup). Restoring onto a new phone may put your data back.

Your rights and how to use them

Your data is on your phone. You control it directly:

  • See your data: it is shown in the app (history, map, trips).
  • Delete it: in Android settings, Apps > EcoDrive OBD > Storage > Clear data (this erases everything the app has saved). Or uninstall the app: the app's private storage is then erased. The tile cache is erased the same way.
  • Remove location, Bluetooth or notifications: Android settings, Apps > EcoDrive OBD > Permissions.

What remains outside the app after deletion: any Android backup (see above), to be deleted from your Google account, and the logs held by Open-Meteo, OpenStreetMap and Google, which are covered by their own policies.

We do not hold your data. If you write to us to ask for access or deletion, we can only point you to these steps. To exercise your rights with the third-party services, write to them directly.

What the app does not do

  • No account, no user ID.
  • No ads, no advertising ID.
  • No trackers, no analytics, no crash reporting.
  • Your driving data is not sent to other drivers. The "community" leaderboard shown in the app is a simulation: the other drivers are generated on your phone and do not exist.

This page and the project website

This page sets no cookie, uses no analytics and loads no resource from another site (no font, no script). As with any website, its host, GitHub (GitHub Pages), receives each visitor's IP address and applies its own policy: https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement (read on 16 October 2026).

The app is not aimed at children. We collect no data about them.
